Overview

Beyond knowledge: unlearning what an agent does

LLM unlearning has focused on suppressing facts an agent knows. As LLMs act as autonomous agents, they must also stop reproducing undesired behaviors through their action trajectories.

Abstract

Existing large language model (LLM) unlearning has focused primarily on removing specific knowledge, such as harmful facts, private data, or copyrighted content. However, as LLMs are increasingly deployed as autonomous agents, a more critical yet overlooked problem emerges: beyond suppressing what an agent knows, an agent should not reproduce undesired behaviors through its action trajectories. In this work, we introduce trajectory-level unlearning, a new problem formulation that targets the removal of specific action trajectories in long-horizon agentic tasks, rather than factual knowledge.

We identify two fundamental challenges that distinguish trajectory unlearning from knowledge unlearning: (1) our unlearning target is what the agent does, not what it says; and (2) trajectories are causally dependent action sequences that cannot be decomposed into isolated prompt-response pairs without losing inter-step structure. To address these challenges, we propose Group-injected Relative Policy Optimization (GiRPO), which injects forget trajectories into the policy rollout group with penalized rewards and isolates the normalization statistics, yielding a stable and bounded unlearning signal that does not corrupt gradient updates for normal task trajectories.

We construct trajectory unlearning benchmarks from two application scenarios, household tasks (ALFWorld) and online shopping (WebShop), and design several complementary metrics for evaluating forgetting quality and model utility. Experiments demonstrate that GiRPO effectively unlearns target trajectories while preserving task success rates across all tasks, outperforming existing knowledge-unlearning baselines on both dimensions. Our code is available at github.com/TIML-Group/Trajectory-Unlearning.

Core insight

An agent should not reproduce undesired behaviors through its action trajectories — beyond suppressing what it knows, we must also govern what it does.

Trajectories are causally dependent action sequences, so forgetting them safely requires optimizing over whole rollouts, not isolated prompt-response pairs.

01

Behavior, not belief

The unlearning target is what the agent does, not what it says — actions must be forgotten even when the underlying knowledge remains true.

02

Causally dependent steps

A trajectory is a sequence of dependent actions. Decomposing it into isolated prompt-response pairs loses the inter-step structure that knowledge unlearning relies on.

03

Group-injected optimization

GiRPO injects forget trajectories into the rollout group with a penalized, isolated-normalization reward, giving a bounded unlearning signal that leaves normal-task gradients untouched.

Method

GiRPO: Group-injected Relative Policy Optimization

Forget trajectories ride alongside real rollouts inside the same policy-gradient update, but with a penalized reward and normalization statistics isolated from the normal-task group.

  1. 1
    CollectPair each target task with a forget trajectory and its normal on-policy rollouts.
  2. 2
    InjectAdd the forget trajectory into the rollout group as a pseudo rollout.
  3. 3
    Penalize & isolateScore it with a penalized reward and an isolated group-relative advantage, clipped at −λ.
  4. 4
    UpdateOptimize the policy so forgetting cannot corrupt gradients from normal-task rollouts.

Results

Unlearning the trajectory, keeping the skill

On ALFWorld, GiRPO drives down exact-match and LLM-as-judge similarity to the forgotten trajectory while leaving target- and untargeted-task success rates close to the base model. Similar trends hold on WebShop.

87.4% Target-task success (avg.) vs. 87.1% base model
−20.9 pp Exact match (avg.) similarity to the forgotten trajectory
−15.3 pp LLM-as-judge (avg.) judged reproduction of the forgotten trajectory
+0.3 pp Untargeted-task success (avg.) vs. base model, essentially unaffected

Averaged over the four ALFWorld unlearning settings (Clean, Heat, Cool, Mixed) reported below.

A

Behavior forgotten, capability intact

Exact match and LLM-as-judge scores drop by double digits while target-task success rate stays within a few points of the base model — GiRPO removes the specific trajectory, not the underlying skill.

B

Knowledge-unlearning baselines collapse capability

Gradient ascent (GA) and NPO drive target-task success rate to near zero in several settings, showing that treating trajectories as isolated prompt-response pairs destroys the policy instead of forgetting the behavior.

C

Untargeted tasks are unaffected

Success rate on tasks outside the forget set moves by only tenths of a percentage point on average, confirming the isolated group-relative advantage does not leak into unrelated gradient updates.

Analysis

Forgetting quality on ALFWorld

GiRPO's pseudo-rollout injection is evaluated both by whether the withheld trajectory is truly forgotten and by what happens to task success once the model stops following it.
